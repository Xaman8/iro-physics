# IRO Envelope Model: Formal Specification (working)

Vocabulary follows "Grounding Industrial System Ontologies in Physics" (Arseneau & Arseneau, draft 2026.10.27).
Proposed additions are marked **[PROPOSED]**. Changes to earlier steps are recorded in the Change Log.

## Step 1: Asset as a physical behaviour (LOCKED 2026-11-19)

- Asset i has state x_i and stored energy H_i(x_i).
- **Port** (paper): an interface where energy crosses a boundary; typed by domain (mechanical, fluid, thermal, electrical, field) with an effort/flow pair; power = e_p f_p. Ports are direction-neutral.
- **Coupling** (paper): reified relation linking a **source port** to a **target port**; carries nominal direction. Actual direction at time t is the sign of the coupling power P_c(t).
- Energy balance:

  dH_i/dt = sum_{c: target(c) in i} P_c  -  sum_{c: source(c) in i} P_c  -  D_i,   D_i >= 0

- Internal entropy generation: dS_gen,i/dt = D_i / T_i + sum_h Q_h (1/T_cold,h - 1/T_hot,h) (Prigogine d_iS), with T in kelvin at the port where the loss lands, and the second sum over heat Q_h that crosses a temperature difference inside the asset. **[LOCKED TEXT CORRECTED 2026-11-19, red team; see Change Log. Was: "dS_gen,i/dt = D_i / T_i".]**
- State-space form: dx_i/dt = (J_i - R_i) grad H_i + G_i u_i,  y_i = G_i^T grad H_i.
- **Five ports per asset (LOCKED amendment 2026-11-19, Brant).** Every asset has exactly five ports, one per domain: mechanical, fluid, thermal, electrical and chemical. Ports the asset does not use are present but empty (zero power, no coupling). This replaces "field" in the domain list above with "chemical" for asset ports. Open detail: how one port per domain represents an asset with two same-domain terminals today (valve inlet/outlet, gearbox in/out shafts, the two streams of a heat exchanger). Toy model v2 does not yet follow this rule (71 ports over 32 assets, four domains, no chemical domain); see DESCRIBED_MODEL.md.
- **[PROPOSED] Port directionality**: design constraint of a port, one of {source-only, target-only, reversible}. Not the physics of direction; it states what the port is built to tolerate. P_c(t) against a port's design directionality is an envelope excursion (failure signal).


## Step 2: Couplings (LOCKED 2026-11-19)

- Coupling c links source port s(c) to target port t(c) (paper). Medium/structure is a property of the coupling.
- 2a Energy accounting: P_c^s - P_c^t = D_c >= 0; dS_gen,c/dt = D_c / T_c + Q_c (1/T_t - 1/T_s), where Q_c is heat carried from a source port at T_s to a target port at T_t (zero for work-domain couplings). Ideal direct-port coupling D_c = 0. Heat conduction has D_c = 0 but positive entropy generation when T_s > T_t. **[LOCKED TEXT CORRECTED 2026-11-19, red team; see Change Log. Was: "dS_gen,c/dt = D_c / T_c".]**
- 2b Constitutive law (Onsager): J = L X, L symmetric (L12 = L21), L positive semidefinite; entropy production sigma_c = X^T L X >= 0.
- 2c Coupling strength (Kedem-Caplan): q_c = L12 / sqrt(L11 L22), |q_c| <= 1. Weight for envelope detection (Step 5) and energy-weighted distance (Step 7).
- 2d Topologies (paper): direct port (stiff, ~lossless), medium-only (lossy, delayed), structure-mediated (weak, often unexpected cross-envelope path).
- 2e **Domain conversion lives inside the asset** (decided 2026-11-19): assets own ports of different domains; couplings join same-domain ports. Domain-crossing distance = number of converting assets on a path.
- 2f **Spectral description** [PROPOSED]: each port/coupling carries power spectral density and cross-spectral density with other ports (phase kept). Each asset carries resonance data (natural frequencies, damping) in its design envelope.


## Step 3: The plant (LOCKED 2026-11-19)

- 3a Graph G = (A, C): assets = nodes, couplings = edges between ports. Incidence B_ic = +1 if asset i holds target port of c, -1 if source port, 0 otherwise. dH/dt (vector) = B P_C - D_A.
- 3b Port-Hamiltonian form: x' = (J(x,theta) - R(x,theta)) grad H + G_b u_b, y_b = G_b^T grad H, H = sum_i H_i. J skew (lossless interconnection), R >= 0 (all dissipation incl. Onsager L blocks), theta(t) = operating state.
- 3c dH/dt = y_b^T u_b - grad H^T R grad H; d_iS/dt = sum_i dS_gen,i/dt + sum_c dS_gen,c/dt >= 0, each including its heat-transfer term (Steps 1 and 2a). Maintenance = entropy export (negative d_eS), per paper. **[LOCKED TEXT CORRECTED 2026-11-19, red team; see Change Log. Was: "d_iS/dt = sum_i D_i/T_i + sum_c D_c/T_c >= 0".]**
- 3d J and R depend on theta: coupling graph, envelopes and cross-envelope failure paths can be operating-mode dependent (e.g. startup).
- 3e Plant boundary (decided 2026-11-19): reservoir, ambient, grid, fuel supply are boundary ports backed by ideal sources (fixed effort), not assets. "Asset" = something owned and maintained. Same boundary notion reused for envelopes.
- 3f **[PROPOSED] note: all-pairs bound versus actual couplings** (2026-11-19, not locked; 3a-3e unchanged). With the Step 1 five-port amendment, N assets have N_ports = 5N ports, so a model in which every port may act on every other has (5N)^2 = 25N^2 port-to-port entries: 2.5 x 10^9 at N = 10,000. Actual plants have a few couplings per asset. v2 has 50 couplings over 32 assets (mean coupling degree 3.1, max 6). The 10,016-asset benchmark plant (tiled v2, `iro-toy-model/v2/scaling/SCALING.md`) has 15,650 couplings plus 590 ties, and its Y(s) has about 7.4 nonzeros per asset. The benchmark used v2's port set (about 2.2 ports per asset, 22,223 ports at 10,016 assets), not 5N. An empty port has no coupling and no injection, so it adds no off-diagonal entry; 5N is the ontology count, and the solved system needs only the ports in use. Per operating state, the plant is therefore a sparse linear system. Measured on one CPU core (no GPU was tested): one update in about 20 ms at 10,016 assets with stored factorizations (19.8 ms in SCALING.md Table 3; a spot check after the 2026-11-19 red-team fixes gave 19.3 ms, and the full sweep has not been rerun), with near-linear growth for local couplings and steeper growth for long-range meshing. Consequence: on the plants measured, envelopes (Step 5, Step 6b) are not justified by single-machine speed; their claimed value is scaling to large or meshed plants, cheap local updates, recursion and revealing multi-asset behaviour (lead hypothesis 2), none yet measured. Hypothesis (8) [PROPOSED] (Brant, 2026-11-19) states the computing reason for envelopes in large or tightly meshed plants; XB and X4 are to test it.

## Step 5: Envelope discovery [PROPOSED] (2026-11-19, not locked)

Envelopes are found from the plant's physics, not assigned. Tested in toy model v2 (`iro-toy-model/v2/discover_envelopes.py`).

- 5a Coupling weights, each computed from the Step 3 plant alone: (i) design power flow |P_c| per coupling; (ii) **[PROPOSED]** Kron two-port coupling ratio (earlier called the Kedem-Caplan degree of coupling), q_c(w) = |H12| / sqrt(H11 H22), where Y is the full plant Y(jw) reduced onto the two ports of coupling c, per band w, and H = (Y + Y^H)/2 is its Hermitian part; q_c <= 1 whenever H is positive semidefinite, and it is clipped at 1 otherwise (no pair needed clipping in v2). It plays the role of Step 2c on the whole plant, but it is not Onsager's L, because the plant has non-reciprocal heat bonds. Corrected 2026-11-19 after the red team: the earlier formula |Y12| / sqrt(|Y11||Y22|) is not bounded and reached 1.004, 1.058 and 2.374 in v2 at 1, 6 and 150 rad/s; the bounded ratio peaks at 0.987; (iii) timescale signatures (participation factors per timescale decade, Perez-Arriaga et al.); (iv) slow coherency (Winkelman et al.).
- 5b Partitioning: normalised spectral clustering (k from the eigengap) and Louvain modularity, on the asset graph with weights from 5a.
- 5c Scores reported with every partition: eigengap, modularity Q, **null models** (weight shuffle and degree-preserving rewiring; modularity alone does not show envelopes, Guimera et al. 2004), stability under +/-10 % and +/-30 % parameter perturbation (ARI), sensitivity to the parasitic-coupling rule, strong-inside/weak-between ratio of q, and ARI against the engineer's grouping.
- 5d Selection rule (fixed before scoring against the engineer): among the power-flow and coupling-ratio (5a ii) partitions, the one with the highest mean ARI under +/-30 % parameter perturbation; ties go to higher Q, then the DC weighting.
- 5e Result on v2 (32 assets): partly emergent. After the 2026-11-19 red-team fixes the rule picks the coupling-ratio Louvain partition at 1 rad/s (KCw1-louvain): 7 envelopes that follow energy chains (mean ARI 0.98, minimum 0.87, under +/-30 % perturbation; ARI 0.32 vs the engineer's functional grouping). Power-flow Louvain still gives the same 5 groups as before (ARI 0.43 vs the engineer), but its stability fell from 1.0 to 0.91. Methods agree only partly: ARI 0.22-0.64 between the PF and coupling-ratio partitions, 0.16-0.43 against the engineer (k forced to 4 excluded). Power-flow Q is below its weight-shuffle null; the chosen partition's Q is 0.66 against a null of 0.62 +/- 0.03 (p = 0.04); topology Q against rewired graphs gives p = 0.16. The coupling ratio is strong inside and weak between (median 0.84 inside vs 0.31 between for the chosen envelopes). Before the fixes 5e read: 5 PF-louvain envelopes, ARI 1.0 under perturbation, 0.43 vs the engineer, methods 0.24-0.55 (the data then gave 0.22-0.48), q 0.85 inside vs 0.28 between.
- 5f Each discovered envelope is reduced by Step 6b to an envelope-asset (boundary ports, Z_E(s), Norton equivalent, reconstruction map) and couples to the others through the unchanged cross-envelope couplings. In the linear model this is exact (checked to 1.5e-14) and every reduced envelope is passive. Passive after reduction does not mean passive inside: in v2 the gas-train envelope's own admittance has a negative Hermitian eigenvalue (-1.9e-3, at C1.thermal / IC1.thermal), caused by the one-way heat bonds (open item, 2026-11-19).
- 5h **[PROPOSED] expectation, testable hypothesis (Brant, 2026-11-19):** an asset typically uses 1-3 of its five ports, but as assets aggregate into envelopes the envelope's boundary ports are expected to span all five domains. Not a result. In v2 the seven envelopes chosen after the 2026-11-19 fixes span 1, 2, 4, 2, 3, 4 and 4 domains (Supply, MCC1 pumps + fan, Drives, CW pump 1, Reactor + heat rejection, Oil + bearing, Gas train; before the fixes the five PF-louvain envelopes spanned 2, 3, 4, 2 and 4); five is impossible in v2 because it has no chemical domain. Test needs a plant with chemical paths and the five-port rule applied. **v3 result (2026-11-19, made-up plant with chemical paths and the five-port rule, `iro-toy-model/v3/`): not supported.** No designed or discovered envelope boundary spans all five domains. Counting couplings to other envelopes, the five designed L1 envelopes span 1, 4, 4, 3 and 3 domains (ELEC, AREA_A, AREA_B, UTIL, HEAT); HEAT reaches 4 when its plant boundary ports are counted. The six discovered L1 envelopes span 1-4 domains (2-4 with plant boundary ports); before the 2026-11-19 fixes there were ten, spanning 1-3. In the designed tree, mechanical couplings never cross an L1 or L2 boundary, only L3 boundaries that split a shaft; chemical crosses level 1 only between AREA_A and AREA_B (reactor to product tank). Most assets use 2 of 5 ports (73 of 99), and aggregation adds domains only up to the four that move between areas (electrical, fluid, thermal, chemical). Single made-up plant; one counter-example, not a general result.
- 5g Open: operating-mode dependence (3d, RQ13); recursion (RQ3); choice between weights when they disagree.
- 5i **[PROPOSED] Recursive discovery (2026-11-19, tested in toy model v3):** apply 5a-5d at level 1, then run Louvain again inside each discovered envelope on the sub-block of the same plant-wide weight matrix. Split a block only if it has at least MIN_SPLIT = 6 assets and the split has Q >= Q_MIN = 0.25; stop at depth 3. The stop rule is a choice, not derived. v3 result after the 2026-11-19 red-team fixes: level 1 picks KCw0.01-spectral; 6 / 18 / 3 envelopes at L1 / L2 / L3, ARI vs the designed tree 0.51 / 0.53 / 0.53, stability under +/-30 % perturbation 0.99 / 0.87 / 0.87 (mean ARI). Before the fixes: KCw0.01-louvain, 10 / 17 / 2 envelopes, ARI 0.37 / 0.51 / 0.54, stability 0.99 / 0.87 / 0.88.

## Failure mechanism: Convergent excitation [PROPOSED] (2026-11-19)

Two or more sources, possibly in different envelopes, each within limits, whose combined response at a target asset exceeds its limit (in-phase superposition, frequency near resonance, or nonlinear mixing w1 +/- w2). Damage is computed on the combined response (fatigue ~ A^m), never summed per source. Averaged-power (SEA-style) envelope summaries cannot detect it.

## Step 6b (planned): Port-to-port reduction [PROPOSED]

- Eliminate internal states (Schur complement / Kron reduction) to get a port-to-port map y(s) = Z(s) u(s), valid per operating state (linearised).
- |Z_jk| at the fault band = transfer-attenuation distance.
- Derived view for fast screening only; the coupling graph remains the model because the map loses path/attribution, nonlinearity and damage in eliminated assets.

## Step 6c: Recursive reduction [PROPOSED] (2026-11-19, not locked)

Envelopes of envelopes (RQ3). Tested in toy model v3 (`iro-toy-model/v3/hierarchy.py`, `reduction_v3.py`).

- 6c-1 A hierarchy is a tree: plant > level-1 envelopes > level-2 envelopes > ... > assets. Every asset sits in exactly one leaf. A node's boundary ports are its ports that couple to something outside the node, plus any plant boundary ports inside it.
- 6c-2 Reduce bottom-up. For a leaf, Kron/Schur-reduce its assets' nodal block Y(s) onto its boundary ports, giving Y_E(s) and the Norton current J_E. For a parent, assemble the reduced children with the couplings between them (the sibling couplings, which stay as couplings), then reduce onto the parent's boundary ports.
- 6c-3 In the linear model this equals one-shot reduction of the same node, because Schur complements compose. Passivity carries up the tree only if its premise holds: a Schur complement of a matrix whose Hermitian part is >= 0 keeps that property, provided Y_II is invertible. A passive reduced matrix does not prove the node is passive inside, so the check now also tests each node's own Y and its Y_II (added 2026-11-19 after the red team). v3 check after the fixes: max relative error 2.8e-13 (designed tree, 48 nodes) and 9.6e-14 (discovered tree, 27 nodes) at 36 frequencies from DC to 159 Hz; every reduced node passive, some marginally (lossless at DC); Y_II invertible everywhere (smallest singular value 1.6e-4, condition number up to 5.5e12 from the spread of thermal capacitances). Inside, one designed node fails: the burner envelope HEAT.BLR.BRN, minimum eigenvalue -0.0095 at BN-H1.thermal, because the one-way fuel-to-heat bond is a controlled source. Recorded as an open item, not tuned away. Before the fixes: 3.4e-13 and 1.8e-13, 29 discovered nodes, inside not checked.
- 6c-4 The reduced node is used exactly like an asset (Step 6, envelope-as-asset) by its parent. Faults are traced down the tree with the reconstruction maps of each level.
- 6c-5 Limits: linear, one operating state; not tested for nonlinear or mode-switching plants. No speed benefit measured (RQ16).

## Five-port rule: same-domain terminals [PROPOSED] (2026-11-19, not locked)

Answers the open detail in the Step 1 five-port amendment, as v3 implements it. Locked Step 1 text unchanged.

- Merge: when two same-domain terminals carry the same stream through the asset (pipe or valve inlet and outlet, a header, a knock-out drum, a stack), they become one port. The pressure drop sits on the couplings either side.
- Split: when the asset holds two different streams in one domain (the two sides of a heat exchanger, the gas and water sides of a boiler), it is modelled as two assets joined by a coupling.
- Cost seen in v3: 9 of 99 assets have zero nominal sigma, because their whole loss sits on couplings or boundary sinks, so a sigma-based Safe limit cannot be set for them (recounted after the 2026-11-19 fixes: still the same 9).
- v3 now uses T_amb = 298.15 K as the thermal effort base, as v2 does (changed 2026-11-19 after the red team; it was 1000 K, and v2 was 100 K). The 1000 K base existed to keep the chemical-to-thermal burner a reciprocal transformer with modulus <= 1. The burner is now a one-way heat bond, so the base no longer has to keep efforts below 1, and T = T_amb (1 + e) gives sigma in kW/K.
