Inverted Reliability Ontology

Can a failure be predicted from energy that started somewhere else in the plant, however the cause and effect are separated?

IRO Physics models a plant as assets that pass energy to each other through ports and couplings. It groups those assets into envelopes and reduces each envelope to one equivalent asset, because in a large or tightly meshed plant the graph, sparse as it is, is still very large, and running the whole thing to predict behaviour and faults in real time would take too much computing. That is our hypothesis, and it has not been tested yet. Entropy generation stands in for damage. The work so far runs on two made-up toy plants, not real plant data. The first has 11 assets in 4 envelopes set by function. The second has 32 assets, 71 ports and 50 couplings, and its 5 envelopes are found from coupling strength rather than assigned.

In the first plant, pulsation from a pump in one envelope wears out a heat exchanger in another. Both the full model and the envelope model traced the damage back to the pump in all 40 runs, about 62 days before failure. A local sensor on the heat exchanger warned just as early, but it never named the source. In the second plant, the models named the cross-envelope cause about 165 days ahead, with no false alarms in 40 fault-free runs. Local trending raised a false alarm in all 40 of those runs. The models still have limits: one fault looks the same as another at the envelope boundary, combined excitation is over-predicted, and in one scenario the wrong failing asset was picked. A scaling benchmark on one core has not borne the hypothesis out so far. Solving every asset with sparse math took about 20 ms per update at 10,000 assets, and the envelope version gave the same answer but was no faster. The cost did climb on a tightly meshed plant, to 11.6 s per update at 100,000 assets with every factorization redone, against 25.2 s for the envelope version, so the test now moves to larger and more meshed plants and to updates where only one envelope changes. Revealing behaviour that involves several assets at once is a separate case for envelopes.

Next steps: give every asset five ports, test whether calculus on the instruments detects the faults the model predicts, and compare a full every-asset baseline against the envelope model on the same faults, for accuracy and for computing cost.
