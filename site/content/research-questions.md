# IRO Envelope Work: Research Questions Log

Status: OPEN / IN PROGRESS / ANSWERED / DROPPED. Raised = date and by whom.

## Lead research questions and hypotheses (LOCKED 2026-11-19, Brant)
These are questions (1) and (2) of the study. The RQ0 to RQ14 entries below keep their IDs as stable references and sit under these two.

**(1) Research question.** Can we model how a physical effect in one asset is transferred to and affects another asset, regardless of the distance between them?

**(1) Hypothesis.** Energy transfer modelled port to port through couplings predicts the effect on the receiving asset, whether the two are adjacent or many hops apart.

**(2) Research question.** Does grouping assets into natural nested envelopes keep those asset-to-asset relationships intact, and does it reveal multi-asset behaviour that pairwise analysis misses?

**(2) Hypothesis.** Envelope reduction keeps the cause-and-effect path between assets, and it exposes combined behaviour, such as convergent excitation, that no single asset pair shows.

## Primary question
**RQ0. Cross-envelope failure prediction.** Can we predict the failure of a physical asset in one envelope that was caused by energy transferred from another envelope? (2026-11-19, Brant. IN PROGRESS: toy model.) **Update 2026-11-19 (v2 failure test, 20 seeds x 6 scenarios):** model-based predictors named the cross-envelope root cause (CT1 heat-rejection loss, HX1/HX2 failing) with ~165 d lead and 0 false alarms in 40 no-fault runs. Local trending alarmed in 40/40 no-fault runs and cannot name a remote cause. Limits: the failing asset was wrong in F1 (parametric fault fitted as injection). Convergent excitation (F4) was detected but over-predicted when the true mode was detuned. Under discovered envelopes only 4/20 F1 runs were cross-envelope, versus 20/20 under the engineer's grouping.

## Envelopes
**RQ1. Emergent envelopes.** Do envelopes arise naturally from energy-transfer structure and dynamics (coupling strength, timescale separation), without being imposed? Which detection methods agree? (2026-11-19, Brant. OPEN: NOT YET TESTED. Toy model v1 assigned envelopes by function; automatic discovery (Step 5) not built. Results so far are on chosen envelopes.) **Update 2026-11-19 (toy model v2, 32 assets; status now IN PROGRESS, PARTLY ANSWERED):** Step 5 discovery is built with four physics weightings: power flow, Kedem-Caplan q on Kron two-ports, timescale signatures and slow coherency. The PF-Louvain envelopes (5 envelopes) are stable (ARI 1.0 under +/-30 % parameter perturbation and KAPPA 0.01-0.2) and follow energy chains (drive train with feeder, lube-oil loop, gas train, heat-rejection trunk). They agree only moderately with the engineer's functional grouping (ARI 0.43; other methods 0.28-0.55; timescale methods ~0, since they find domain/timescale layers). Methods agree with each other only partly (ARI 0.24-0.48). Modularity is not above null models (topology Q 0.52 vs rewired 0.49 +/- 0.02, p 0.16). Kedem-Caplan q is clearly strong inside and weak between envelopes (median 0.85 vs 0.28). Answer so far: partly emergent. Coupling strength gives reproducible, interpretable groups, but the number and edges depend on the weighting, and graph scores alone do not separate them from chance. Single operating point only (see RQ13).
**RQ2. Canonical envelope physics.** Can an envelope's internal physics be integrated into a canonical reduced set (storage, dissipation, port relations) so it couples to other envelopes as if it were a physical asset, preserving energy conservation and passivity? (2026-11-19, Brant. IN PROGRESS.) **Update 2026-11-19 (v2):** each of the 5 discovered envelopes is reduced by Kron/Schur to boundary ports + Z_E(s) + Norton equivalent + reconstruction map. Exact in the linear model (error ~1e-15) and passive (min eig Re Y_red >= 0 on a frequency grid; two envelopes are marginal, ~0). Not yet tested on nonlinear or mode-switching plants.
**RQ3. Recursion.** Can envelopes of envelopes be formed with the same reduction? (2026-11-19. OPEN.)
**RQ4. What reductions lose.** Path/attribution, nonlinearity, damage in eliminated assets, phase and frequency content. (2026-11-19, Brant. OPEN.)

## Distance and scalability (linked)
**RQ5. Distance types between initiating and affected asset.** Structural (physical, asset hops, envelope hops, domain crossings); energy (energy-weighted resistance, transfer attenuation |Z_jk|, propagation delay); spectral proximity to target resonances; causal/information lag from data. Which predict effect size, damage rate, lead time? Which need only structure vs data? (2026-11-19, Brant: "not sure all of the types yet". IN PROGRESS.) **Update 2026-11-19 (v2, 12 sources x 31 targets, Spearman vs log effect):** transfer attenuation -0.56 pooled (-0.63 within source; partly circular), domain crossings -0.45, asset hops -0.34, discovered-envelope hops -0.30, physical -0.28, line length -0.28, -log q path -0.24, engineer-envelope hops -0.21, |Z| distance -0.12. Discovered-envelope hops beat engineer-envelope hops. No structural distance is reliable within every source (within-source ranges cross zero for most).
**RQ12. Scalability of mapping.** Is asset-to-asset mapping practical in a large plant? (2026-11-19, Brant. PARTLY ANSWERED: full all-to-all N^2 map impractical; sparse hierarchy: full maps within envelopes, envelope-to-envelope map, selective long-range entries, on-demand back-tracing.) **Linked to RQ5 (Brant, 2026-11-19):** distances are the filters that decide which pairs to compute. Open: which combination of distances gives a filter that misses almost no real failure paths (spectral proximity needed because hops-only misses convergent excitation). **Update 2026-11-19 (v2):** an envelope-to-envelope model with sensors only at the 29 boundary ports (of 71) matched the full model on lead time, root cause and false alarms for faults whose source is identifiable at the boundary. It lost intra-envelope identifiability: a C1 surge was blamed on M6 in the same envelope, halving lead time (71 vs 142 d). So the sparse hierarchy needs enough sensing inside each envelope to separate its own sources, and the reduced map is exact only in a linear model. **Update 2026-11-19 (scaling benchmark, `iro-toy-model/v2/scaling/SCALING.md`; Brant: "can we calculate the complexity and time to solve it?"):** solving every asset as a baseline is practical for the linear lumped model if the solver exploits sparsity. Measured on one core (Xeon, 8-core box, 15 GB), with tiled v2 plants from 32 to 100,000 assets. One full update (operating point + 5 fault bands + sigma and 3 derivatives for every asset + online root-cause scoring) takes 20 ms at 10k and 0.27 s at 100k with stored factorizations, or 0.14 s and 1.6 s when everything is refactorized. A plant-year at the 6-h v2 sample takes 7-38 min at 100k. Sparse cost is near-linear (exponent 1.0-1.1) when the coupling graph has small separators (local couplings). Random long-range meshing raises it (local exponent ~2.3 between 30k and 100k; refactorized update 11.6 s at 100k). Three things do not scale: dense algebra (O(n^3), 788 GB at 100k), exact offline attribution tables (O(N^2), 3.4 h for 5 bands at 100k), and time-domain simulation that resolves the 25 Hz bands (~2e10 steps per plant-year, years of compute; avoided by using phasors). The envelope approach (per-envelope Kron + boundary solve) is exact but was not faster than a good sparse direct solver on one core. Sparse elimination already performs the same Schur complements. Its expected advantages are parallel offline work, cheap local updates when one envelope changes, and recursion (RQ3); these are not yet measured. **Step 4 consequence (2026-11-19, eight-step framing):** because the full sparse solve is already fast (about 20 ms per update at 10k assets on one core), envelopes are not justified by single-machine speed. Their case rests on scaling to large or densely meshed plants, cheap local updates, recursion (RQ3) and revealing multi-asset behaviour (lead hypothesis (2)). See RQ16.

## Ports and couplings
**RQ6. Port-to-port mapping.** Can every port map directly to every other, skipping intermediate transformations? (2026-11-19, Brant. PARTLY ANSWERED: yes for a linearised plant per operating state via Schur complement / Kron reduction Z(s); loses path, nonlinearity, intermediate damage. Screening view only.)
**RQ7. Port direction.** Port or coupling? (2026-11-19. ANSWERED: coupling; port directionality is a design constraint [PROPOSED]. See FORMAL_MODEL.md change log.)
**RQ8. Where domain conversion lives.** Inside the asset or as transforming couplings? (2026-11-19. ANSWERED by Brant 2026-11-19: inside the asset; couplings join same-domain ports.)

## Combination effects
**RQ9. Convergent excitation.** Can individually nominal inputs (e.g. vibrations) from different envelopes combine at a target to hit a resonance or exceed a damage limit, and can each reduction detect it? Needs spectral/cross-spectral port descriptions and per-asset resonance data; damage on combined response (fatigue ~ A^m); includes nonlinear mixing (w1 +/- w2). (2026-11-19, Brant. IN PROGRESS: toy-model test approved 2026-11-19.) **Update 2026-11-19 (v2, F4):** P1 1x steady at 0.6 of the endurance amplitude, plus P2 1x rising to 0.85 at an HDR1 mode. The full and both reduced models named P2 and HDR1 correctly in all 10 failing runs, but also alarmed in 9-10 of the 10 runs where the true (detuned) plant never failed. Near a resonance, the design modal parameters are not accurate enough, so they need identification from data.

**RQ13. Mode dependence.** Do envelopes and cross-envelope failure paths change with operating mode theta (startup, trips, valve states)? (2026-11-19, from Step 3. OPEN.)

## Damage
**RQ10. Damage measure.** Internal dissipation only (entropy generation, Bryant et al. 2008), or also port power excursions beyond design (amount, direction, frequency)? (2026-11-19. OPEN.) **Update 2026-11-19 (v2):** sigma alone missed temperature ageing (thermal ports) and oscillatory fatigue (combined modal amplitude). v2 uses three coordinates (sigma, temperature, fatigue) under one uniform margin rule. Evidence that internal dissipation alone is not enough.

**RQ15. Does instrument calculus detect predicted faults?** Running the value, rate, acceleration and jerk charts (derivative orders 0 to 3) on the instrument readings: do they detect a fault the physics model predicts, and how early? Planned test: compare three detectors on the same fault and no-fault runs: (a) the model's prediction, (b) instrument calculus alone, (c) the model's band matching. Measures: correct root cause, lead time and false alarms. (2026-11-19, Brant. OPEN: not built; derivatives are display only today.)

Full baseline versus envelopes.** Is a full all-asset baseline solve sufficient on its own, and what do envelopes add beyond it (accuracy, multi-asset behaviour, local updates, meshed plants)? (2026-11-19, from the eight-step framing, step 4. OPEN.
