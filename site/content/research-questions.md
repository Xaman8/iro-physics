## Lead research questions and hypotheses (LOCKED 2026-11-19, Brant)
These are questions (1) and (2) of the study. The RQ0 to RQ14 entries below keep their IDs as stable references and sit under these two.

**(1) Research question (reworded 2026-11-19, approved by Brant; the old wording is in the Change Log of FORMAL_MODEL.md).** Can a transfer map built port to port through the couplings predict how a physical effect in one asset affects another asset, and how does the prediction error change with hop count and with transfer attenuation?

**(1) Hypothesis (reworded 2026-11-19, approved by Brant after red team; the old wording is in the Change Log of FORMAL_MODEL.md).** A transfer map built port to port through the couplings predicts the effect on the receiving asset, whether the two are adjacent or many hops apart, as long as the transferred effect stays above a stated attenuation and signal-to-noise limit. The test is the prediction error at the receiving asset as a function of hop count and of transfer attenuation, with the model's parameters in error and with a coupling that exists in the plant missing from the model. Scope: this hypothesis covers energy carried through ports. Effects also travel through matter (composition, solids), control signals, discrete events and, in SAGD, the reservoir. Those paths are outside this hypothesis and are not yet modelled.

**(2) Research question (reworded 2026-11-19, approved by Brant; the old wording is in the Change Log of FORMAL_MODEL.md).** When assets are grouped into nested envelopes and each envelope is reduced to one equivalent asset, do the envelopes help sensing and reading the plant more than random groupings of the same sizes, judged by isolability and held-out prediction?

**(2) Hypothesis (reworded 2026-11-19, approved by Brant after red team; the old wording is in the Change Log of FORMAL_MODEL.md).** Nested envelopes, each reduced to one equivalent asset with boundary ports and a reconstruction map, work better as a structure for sensing and for reading the plant than random groupings of the same sizes. Two tests, with criteria fixed before the runs: isolability (whether sensors on the envelope boundaries can still tell the candidate sources apart) and held-out prediction (fault-propagation error, or boundary-only attribution accuracy, on faults not used to choose the envelopes). Each is compared with null partitions of the same size distribution. Not claimed: that exact reduction reveals multi-asset behaviour the full model does not already hold (for a linear model it carries nothing beyond Y inverse), or that natural envelopes emerge from the physics. Convergent excitation stays under RQ9.

## Primary question
**RQ0. Cross-envelope failure prediction.** Can we predict the failure of a physical asset in one envelope that was caused by energy transferred from another envelope? (2026-11-19, Brant. IN PROGRESS: toy model.) **Update 2026-11-19 (v2 failure test, 20 seeds x 6 scenarios; rerun after the red-team fixes the same day):** in F1 (cooling-tower CT1 fouling) the full and envelope predictors warned in 20/20 runs, median lead 158-159 d, and named CT1 as the source in 20/20. They named the first failing asset correctly in 11/20: CT1 itself failed first in 11 runs, HX2 in 4, HX1 in 3 and C1 in 2, and the predictors always named CT1. Model predictors raised 0 alarms in 40 no-fault runs (C0 and F5). Local trending alarmed in 39/40 and cannot name a remote cause. Limits: one fault looks like another at the envelope boundary (F3); convergent excitation (F4) was detected but over-predicted when the true mode was detuned. Under the discovered envelopes only 6/20 F1 runs were cross-envelope, versus 9/20 under the engineer's grouping. Before the fixes the same test reported a 165 d lead, the failing asset wrong in 20/20 (HX1 16, HX2 4), local alarms in 40/40, and 4/20 versus 20/20 cross-envelope runs; those figures are superseded. **Update 2026-11-19 (toy model v3, cross-level faults, 20 seeds each, made-up plant):** three faults: CW pump P-U1 to reactor jacket J-A1 (crosses L1), compressor stall K-B1 to aftercooler E-B1G (crosses L2 inside one L1), and a harmonic-filter interharmonic on the 4.16 kV bus (HF1) to an agitator gearbox torsional mode GB-A3 (electrical origin, mechanical victim, L2 in ELEC to L3 in AREA_A). Rerun after the red-team fixes: FULL warned in 20/20 runs of each, with median lead 82-85 d, and named the right failing asset in 20/20. It named the right source in 20/20 for the first two faults, but in only 11/20 for the electrical fault: in 6 it named LR-B1, a line reactor on the same stiff bus, and in 3 another ELEC asset (SWG1 2, TX1 1). Sensing only designed L1 boundaries matched FULL for the L1-crossing fault but warned in 0/20 for the L2 fault. Sensing designed L2 boundaries matched FULL for both. Sensing the discovered L1 boundaries warned in 0/20 for the L2 fault; for the pump fault it warned in 20/20 through the pump motors' electrical feed but named the other CW pump, P-U2 (right envelope, wrong asset), because the source and the victim share discovered envelope D5. It got the electrical source envelope right in 20/20. No model predictor alarmed in 80 no-fault predictor runs or before onset in any fault run. Local trending alarmed in 19/20 no-fault runs. (Before the fixes: FULL lead 85-91 d, electrical source 9/20, L1 sensing 1/20 on the L2 fault, discovered-L1 sensing 0/20 and 1/20, 2 pre-onset alarms.) The temperature-margin rule and three victim modes were changed after a first run in which 9/20 no-fault plants failed (disclosed in the v3 README). **Update 2026-11-19 (red team; decisions approved by Brant):** no current result supports RQ0 yet, because in every test the truth plant is the inference plant with +/-10 % parameter noise: the same graph, the same fault ports and frequencies, the same noise constants and the same damage law. The plan for an independent truth is XT in EXPERIMENTAL_DESIGN_ADDITIONS.md. Attribution will run an isolability pre-test, allow several sources, and answer "not isolable" or "none of the above" when the sensors cannot separate the sources (XI there). The F3 and F1 misses are structural, as is a case in which two DC sources (CT1.thermal and R1.thermal) were attributed to a third port, HX1.thermal, that explains 99.7 % of the residual. Results that involve mechanical faults (F4, F5) do not transfer as stated to vibration faults in real machinery, because the mechanical port carries torsion only.

## Envelopes
**RQ1. Emergent envelopes.** Do envelopes arise naturally from energy-transfer structure and dynamics (coupling strength, timescale separation), without being imposed? Which detection methods agree? (2026-11-19, Brant. OPEN: NOT YET TESTED. Toy model v1 assigned envelopes by function; automatic discovery (Step 5) not built. Results so far are on chosen envelopes.) **Update 2026-11-19 (toy model v2, 32 assets; status now IN PROGRESS, PARTLY ANSWERED):** Step 5 discovery is built with four physics weightings: power flow, the Kron two-port coupling ratio (earlier called Kedem-Caplan q; bounded by 1 since the 2026-11-19 red-team fix), timescale signatures and slow coherency. After the fixes the selection rule picks KCw1-Louvain, 7 envelopes, stable under +/-30 % parameter perturbation (mean ARI 0.98, minimum 0.87) and following energy chains (supply, the MCC1 pumps and fan, the drives, CW pump 1, reactor with heat rejection, the oil and bearing loop, the gas train). It agrees only weakly with the engineer's functional grouping (ARI 0.32). PF-Louvain still finds the same 5 groups (ARI 0.43 vs the engineer), but its stability fell from 1.0 to 0.91. The PF and coupling-ratio partitions score 0.16-0.43 against the engineer (k forced to 4 excluded) and 0.22-0.64 against each other; timescale methods score ~0, since they find domain/timescale layers. Modularity is not convincingly above null models (topology Q 0.52 vs rewired 0.49 +/- 0.02, p 0.16; chosen partition 0.66 vs its weight-shuffle null 0.62 +/- 0.03, p 0.04). The coupling ratio is clearly strong inside and weak between envelopes (median 0.84 vs 0.31). Which partition wins is itself sensitive: a modelling correction that left the PF-Louvain grouping unchanged changed the winner. (Before the fixes: PF-Louvain chosen, ARI 1.0 under perturbation, methods 0.22-0.48, q 0.85 vs 0.28.) Answer so far: partly emergent. Coupling strength gives reproducible, interpretable groups, but the number and edges depend on the weighting, and graph scores alone do not separate them from chance. Single operating point only (see RQ13). **Update 2026-11-19 (toy model v3, 99 assets, nested; made-up plant):** at three levels, discovery agrees only partly with the designed tree (rerun after the red-team fixes: ARI 0.51 / 0.53 / 0.53 at L1 / L2 / L3, was 0.37 / 0.51 / 0.54; 0.77 / 0.63 at L2 / L3 when the recursion runs inside the designed L1 envelopes). The discovered groups follow electrical stiffness and shafts rather than process areas: each drive and MCC sits with its supply transformer and the train it feeds, and the CW pumps, the CW network, the cooling tower, the reactor and its jacket form one group. The coupling ratio is strong inside and weak between at designed L1 and L2 (median 0.73 vs 0.54 and 0.56), but not at designed L3 (0.61 inside vs 0.64 between), where the engineer's split cuts shafts. Hypothesis 5h is not supported in this plant: no envelope boundary spans all five domains (at most four), and mechanical couplings cross only L3 boundaries. Which envelopes are sensed matters for tracing faults: the discovered L1 boundaries hide two of the three test faults (see RQ0). **Update 2026-11-19 (red team; decision approved by Brant):** the claim that natural envelopes emerge from the physics is dropped from hypothesis (2). ARI against the designed tree is self-consistency, because the same author built the plant and the tree, and modularity is at best just above its null models (above: p 0.04 for the chosen v2 partition, p 0.16 for topology). Envelopes are now judged by isolability and held-out prediction against null partitions of the same size distribution. The v3 result on hypothesis 5h above is withdrawn: v3's chemical port is non-physical, so 5h is not reported from v3.
**RQ2. Canonical envelope physics.** Can an envelope's internal physics be integrated into a canonical reduced set (storage, dissipation, port relations) so it couples to other envelopes as if it were a physical asset, preserving energy conservation and passivity? (2026-11-19, Brant. IN PROGRESS.) **Update 2026-11-19 (v2, rerun after the red-team fixes):** each of the 7 discovered envelopes is reduced by Kron/Schur to boundary ports + Z_E(s) + Norton equivalent + reconstruction map. Exact in the linear model (error <= 1.4e-14) and passive after reduction (min eig of the Hermitian part of Y_red >= 0 on a frequency grid; the oil and bearing envelope is marginal, 1.8e-7). Passive after reduction is not passive inside: the gas-train envelope's own admittance has a negative eigenvalue (-1.9e-3, at C1.thermal / IC1.thermal), because the one-way heat bonds are controlled sources. Y_II is invertible in every envelope. Recorded as an open item, not tuned away. Energy conservation holds at design; off design the linear one-way heat bonds leave a first-law gap (3 % of heat-bond power at grid 0.97). Not yet tested on nonlinear or mode-switching plants.
**RQ3. Recursion.** Can envelopes of envelopes be formed with the same reduction? (2026-11-19. OPEN.) **Update 2026-11-19 (toy model v3, made-up plant: 99 assets, plant > 5 L1 > 19 L2 > 24 L3 envelopes, five ports per asset, 14 plant boundary ports in four domains; `iro-toy-model/v3/`; status now IN PROGRESS, PARTLY ANSWERED):** yes in the linear model. Recursive Kron/Schur reduction (reduce each child to its boundary ports, assemble the children with the sibling couplings, reduce again; [PROPOSED] Step 6c) equals one-shot reduction of the same node to round-off at 36 frequencies, DC to 159 Hz. Rerun after the red-team fixes: the designed tree has 48 nodes, max relative error 2.8e-13 in Y and 9.2e-16 in the Norton current. The discovered tree has 27 nodes, 9.6e-14 and 1.5e-15. Every reduced node is passive, some only marginally (lossless at DC). This is expected, since Schur complements compose; the test shows the blocks are assembled correctly at each level. A new inside check tests each node's own admittance: one designed node, the burner envelope HEAT.BLR.BRN, is not passive inside (-0.0095 at BN-H1.thermal) because of the one-way fuel-to-heat bond; every discovered node passes; Y_II is invertible everywhere (condition number up to 5.5e12, from the spread of thermal capacitances). Recursive discovery (v2 discovery, then Louvain inside each discovered envelope on the same plant-wide weights; split only if >= 6 assets and Q >= 0.25) gives 6 / 18 / 3 envelopes at L1 / L2 / L3. ARI against the designed tree is 0.507, 0.531 and 0.532 at L1, L2 and L3. Stability under +/-30 % parameter perturbation is mean ARI 0.99, 0.87 and 0.87. Discovered L1 now matches designed L1 (0.507) better than designed L2 (0.324). (Before the fixes: 29 discovered nodes, errors 3.4e-13 and 1.8e-13; 10 / 17 / 2 envelopes, ARI 0.369 / 0.513 / 0.539, and discovered L1 matched designed L2 better, 0.439, so the earlier "finer at the top" finding does not survive.) Not shown: nonlinear or mode-switching plants, or any speed benefit of recursion (RQ16).

## Distance and scalability (linked)
**RQ5. Distance types between initiating and affected asset.** Structural (physical, asset hops, envelope hops, domain crossings); energy (energy-weighted resistance, transfer attenuation |Z_jk|, propagation delay); spectral proximity to target resonances; causal/information lag from data. Which predict effect size, damage rate, lead time? Which need only structure vs data? (2026-11-19, Brant: "not sure all of the types yet". IN PROGRESS.) **Update 2026-11-19 (v2, 12 sources x 31 targets, Spearman vs log effect; rerun after the red-team fixes):** transfer attenuation -0.90 pooled (-0.76 within source over 11 sources; partly circular), domain crossings -0.36, line length -0.30, asset hops -0.24, -log q path -0.22, |Z| distance -0.21, discovered-envelope hops -0.20, physical -0.17, engineer-envelope hops -0.08. Discovered-envelope hops beat engineer-envelope hops. No structural distance is reliable within every source: within-source ranges cross zero for physical, line length, -log q, |Z| and engineer-envelope hops. (Before the fixes attenuation was -0.56 pooled.) **Update 2026-11-19 (red team):** this measures how effect size varies with distance, not how prediction error does. Lead hypothesis (1) as reworded needs the second: prediction error regressed on hop count and on transfer attenuation, under parameter error and with a coupling missing from the model, with transport delay included.
**RQ12. Scalability of mapping.** Is asset-to-asset mapping practical in a large plant? (2026-11-19, Brant. PARTLY ANSWERED: full all-to-all N^2 map impractical; sparse hierarchy: full maps within envelopes, envelope-to-envelope map, selective long-range entries, on-demand back-tracing.) **Linked to RQ5 (Brant, 2026-11-19):** distances are the filters that decide which pairs to compute. Open: which combination of distances gives a filter that misses almost no real failure paths (spectral proximity needed because hops-only misses convergent excitation). **Update 2026-11-19 (v2):** an envelope-to-envelope model with sensors only at the 28 boundary ports (of 71; 29 before the red-team fixes) matched the full model on lead time, root cause and false alarms for faults whose source is identifiable at the boundary. It lost intra-envelope identifiability: a C1 surge was blamed on M6 in the same envelope, halving lead time (70 vs 140 d). So the sparse hierarchy needs enough sensing inside each envelope to separate its own sources, and the reduced map is exact only in a linear model. **Update 2026-11-19 (scaling benchmark, `iro-toy-model/v2/scaling/SCALING.md`; Brant: "can we calculate the complexity and time to solve it?"):** solving every asset as a baseline is practical for the linear lumped model if the solver exploits sparsity. Measured on one CPU core (Xeon, 8-core box, 15 GB; no GPU was tested), with tiled v2 plants from 32 to 100,000 assets. One full update (operating point + 5 fault bands + sigma and 3 derivatives for every asset + online root-cause scoring) takes 20 ms at 10k and 0.27 s at 100k with stored factorizations, or 0.14 s and 1.6 s when everything is refactorized. A plant-year at the 6-h v2 sample takes 7-38 min at 100k. Sparse cost is near-linear (exponent 1.0-1.1) when the coupling graph has small separators (local couplings). Random long-range meshing raises it (local exponent ~2.3 between 30k and 100k; refactorized update 11.6 s at 100k). Three things do not scale: dense algebra (O(n^3), 788 GB at 100k), exact offline attribution tables (O(N^2), 3.4 h for 5 bands at 100k), and time-domain simulation that resolves the 25 Hz bands (~2e10 steps per plant-year, years of compute; avoided by using phasors). The envelope approach (per-envelope Kron + boundary solve) is exact but was not faster than a good sparse direct solver on one core. Sparse elimination already performs the same Schur complements. Its expected advantages are parallel offline work, cheap local updates when one envelope changes, and recursion (RQ3); these are not yet measured. After the 2026-11-19 red-team fixes a spot check at 10,016 assets gave 19.3 ms cached and 163 ms refactorized (was 19.8 and 142 ms), with the envelope solve exact to 1.2e-14; the full sweep has not been rerun. **Step 4 consequence (2026-11-19, eight-step framing):** because the full sparse solve is already fast (about 20 ms per update at 10k assets on one core), envelopes are not justified by single-machine speed. Their case rests on scaling to large or densely meshed plants, cheap local updates, recursion (RQ3) and revealing multi-asset behaviour (lead hypothesis (2)). See RQ16. (Restated 2026-11-19 after the red team, approved by Brant: the case for envelopes now rests on sensing and interpretability, lead hypothesis (2) as reworded. Speed appears only in hypothesis (8), as a conditional claim that is untested.)

## Combination effects
**RQ9. Convergent excitation.** Can individually nominal inputs (e.g. vibrations) from different envelopes combine at a target to hit a resonance or exceed a damage limit, and can each reduction detect it? Needs spectral/cross-spectral port descriptions and per-asset resonance data; damage on combined response (fatigue ~ A^m); includes nonlinear mixing (w1 +/- w2). (2026-11-19, Brant. IN PROGRESS: toy-model test approved 2026-11-19.) **Update 2026-11-19 (v2, F4):** P1 1x steady at 0.6 of the endurance amplitude, plus P2 1x rising to 0.85 at an HDR1 mode. After the red-team fixes 11 of 20 runs failed; the full and both reduced models named P2 and HDR1 correctly in all 11, but also alarmed in all 9 runs where the true (detuned) plant never failed (before the fixes: 10 failing runs, alarms in 9-10 of the other 10). Near a resonance, the design modal parameters are not accurate enough, so they need identification from data.

## Damage
**RQ10. Damage measure.** Internal dissipation only (entropy generation, Bryant et al. 2008), or also port power excursions beyond design (amount, direction, frequency)? (2026-11-19. ANSWERED 2026-11-19: no, not on its own; see the last update.) **Update 2026-11-19 (v2):** sigma alone missed temperature ageing (thermal ports) and oscillatory fatigue (combined modal amplitude). v2 uses three coordinates (sigma, temperature, fatigue) under one uniform margin rule. Evidence that internal dissipation alone is not enough. **Update 2026-11-19 (red team):** sigma now has units (kW/K, thermal effort base T_amb = 298.15 K), counts heat transfer across a temperature difference, and matches the exact exergy destruction at design (off design within -15 % to +23 % per asset in v3, -2 % to +13 % in v2). It remains a provisional proxy: Bryant ties damage to the specific degradation process, so designed losses are not damage in themselves. **Answer 2026-11-19 (red team; decision approved by Brant): no, not on its own.** σ is kept as a power and energy-balance (efficiency) check. Damage is tracked with mechanism-specific coordinates: Arrhenius temperature, Miner/Basquin fatigue with real stress, L10 or ISO 281 for bearings, corrosion rate and fouling resistance. Per-mechanism entropy with its own coefficient B_i (Bryant, Khonsari and Ling 2008) is used only where a calibrated B_i exists, such as sliding wear and coupon fatigue. Designed and process losses stay out of the damage integral, and Bryant et al. (2008) is no longer cited as support for total asset σ. Not built yet: the failure-test code still uses total σ as one of its damage coordinates.

**RQ16. Full baseline versus envelopes.** Is a full all-asset baseline solve sufficient on its own, and what do envelopes add beyond it (accuracy, multi-asset behaviour, local updates, meshed plants)? (2026-11-19, from the eight-step framing, step 4. OPEN. Known so far: on one core the envelope solve is exact but not faster than a sparse full solve (`iro-toy-model/v2/scaling/SCALING.md`); in the v2 failure test the envelope predictor with boundary-only sensing matched FULL except where internal sources were not identifiable at the boundary (F3). Accuracy, multi-asset behaviour and local-update cost have not been compared on the same scenarios.) **Hypothesis (8) [PROPOSED, UNTESTED] (restated 2026-11-19, approved by Brant after red team; the old wording is in the Change Log of FORMAL_MODEL.md):** approximate envelope models with a stated error bound meet an update deadline D on a plant class P where the exact sparse full solve does not, with D and P fixed before any run. This is a conditional claim to be tested later, not a result. No speed claim is made for exact envelope reduction, and the case for envelopes rests on sensing and interpretability (hypothesis (2)). The benchmark is the reason: on one CPU core (no GPU tested; full sweep from before the 2026-11-19 red-team fixes, spot-checked afterwards) the envelope solve matched the full solve to within 5e-9 relative but was not faster per update at any size measured, including the meshed 100,000-asset plant (refactorized update 11.64 s full, 25.23 s envelope). Tested by XB and X4 (EXPERIMENTAL_DESIGN_ADDITIONS.md): larger and more meshed plants, updates after a change inside one envelope, and a real-time update rate that is still to be set (the v2 predictors use 6-hour means). That rate is the deadline D. The approximate models (weak couplings dropped, balanced truncation, or a rational fit) must be compared with the full solve on the same hardware and in the same language, against METIS or AMD orderings and against Woodbury updates. Even the slowest measured solve, 11.64 s, is about 0.05 % of a 6-hour step.
